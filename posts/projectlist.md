I’ve written a project list. A big to-do list of all the creative projects I want to finish – or at least begin – in the nearby future. Why do I write about this? It felt necessary to reflect on. All my life I have been struggling with finishing what I’ve set out to do. With doing more than just thinking of doing stuff. Now the time has come to check things off this list.

#### 1. Boardgames

A couple of years ago me and my friend met up Tuesdays and made boardgames together. It was mostly a process of finding different mechanics that worked together, through trial and error, with emphasis on the latter. My friend’s concept came the furthest in development. A gladiator fighting and recruiting game – it was honestly quite good. My game, however, I remembered being so bored playing I needed to take a brake. But then, suddenly we stopped, and I discovered our old projects in a box on my bookshelf when I moved. I had filled a good chunk of a notebook annotating in detail my ideas of the processes of my game. It was very intricate. I was close to something, I feel now. I’m going to finish that, and make, yet again, Tuesdays into a boardgame making day.

#### 2. Magazines & Zines

When I visited Hamburg during a hiatus from my biking trip last year, I understood what has to me always been known: I wanted to make my own magazine. Hamburg was filled with zines and magazines, DIYs were littered around, and importantly posters were filling the walls of the city. The city was an outlet for creativity in a sense I believe is reserved for only some European metropoles. I haven’t seen it in Sweden. I liked it a lot.

But there’s also a romanticised picture of magazines I have in terms of reading them. I love going to magazine shops knowing that I can buy bundles of crisp important information. Or rather, you feel important reading them. They have their ear to what’s popular – they decide what’s popular. It’s in this sense also nostalgic, nostalgic for a time I haven’t really witnessed, when that was what magazines really were: trend seekers and trend setters. I’m currently reading a blog from 2005 and 2006 (in book format) and the author speaks of his abundant consumption of magazines as he travels. His irrational desire for magazines. Which I blissfully share.

I should justify this irrational romanticising of them, by doing what I really want: making my own.

#### 3. A dream I had

I played a game in my dream. You controlled a large grappling hook, mounted upon a vehicle with propellers mounted on its rear – heading straight into the heart of a hurricane. With the grappling hook you had to grab onto pirates, following you on a hovering ship. Only when you took all three pirates could you diverge and escape the hurricane. I failed, and my vehicle was consumed. Then, I could see the television where I had played the game, and discovered myself sitting on my sofa of my last apartment, with my dad by my side. I stood up, and inexplicitly grabbed onto a big fan on the roof and let go with my feet. I began to spin, and my body turned into a sky dancer. Twitching and turning as I held onto the blade, it was as if I had been taken by the hurricane myself. In an attempt to wake up, I entered limbo by the logic of dreams – unable to wake up, unable to fall asleep – and my body turned to stone, now completely static, forever spinning around my living room fan.

So vivid its imagery, I thought, I wanted to animate it. And that I shall.

#### 4. Bike light

I don’t know if it’s a universal father-to-son thing, but I believe it might be. Probably it depends on the dad, and the son, but I’m talking about the promise to make something together. A creative project. In most cases a creative project between father and son goes unnoticed, or isn’t registered as such. It’s just what you do together. He fixes something in the apartment, or he helps you install some new tires and break cables on the old commuter bike he gave you when you moved away. Sometimes, however, they are articulated as promises.

One such promise, I now remember, I’ve also had with my grandpa for a number of years. That we are going to make something together at our summer house. Unfortunately, it has yet to happen, and looks very unlikely that it ever will. We live life dreaming of projects.

One promise of a project that has blossomed recently is an idea from my dad: that we make a bike light together. He works with making LEDs, and I have some experience with CAD and explore the idea of studying industrial design. ...and I’m in dire need of a new bike light. Thusly, we concluded that we should make our own, our experience and will combined.

Having said this here, I hope this won’t end up as another dead promise of a great project.
